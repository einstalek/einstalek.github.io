Face Embedding Conditioned Transformer For 3D Face Reconstruction

Here I will describe one of my recent experiments with 3D face reconstruction, based on this awesome Disney paper. Using paper’s transformer decoder architecture, with cross-covariance attention (XCiT) conditioned and modulated with face embeddings, I was able to improve the quality of reconstructed faces. Since I was lacking a sufficiently large 3D head shape dataset, I decided to generate my own dataset using HRN which scores among the highest on 3D Face Reconstruction benchmark.

Shape Transformers and XCiT Blocks#

In their paper, Disney Research proposed a novel approach to modeling 3D shapes using a transformer-based architecture. At the core of their work is the Shape Transformer, which includes:

  • Transformer-based model that’s topology independt, since encoder’s input consists of learned tokens per each point sampled from mesh surface
  • Cross-Covariance Attention (XCiT) blocks are used for learning non-linear spatial correlations

XCiT blocks differ from traditional self-attention mechanisms by operating on the cross-covariance of token features, reducing computational overhead. This makes them ideal for 3D tasks, where input resolution can become a bottleneck. In this particular case, ~5k points is sampled from mesh surface and each one is then translated into a token of dimension 512 (same as face embedding) - so using vanilla self-attention mechanismn would be sort of infisible.

In Shape Transformer, the encoder model gets two 3D inputs, one - points sampled from a canonical shape (averaged head, for example), and the other one - from a target shape. Both inputs are encoded into latent space and passed trough encoder blocks to generate shape code.

Decoder’s input consists of the shape code and points sampled from canonical shape. Decoder’s job is to predict offsets for canonical points.

Both encoder and decoder include several consequitive XCiT blocks.

A Hierarchical Representation Network (HRN)#

HRN is one of the top-performing methods for 3D face reconstruction. It achieves near state-of-the-art results for single photo face reconstruction. The key idea behind HRN is to reconstruct the face shape in a coarse-to-fine manner. First stage includes morphable model prediction (identity and expression) based on the given photo. Second stage aims at refining low-frequency details by predicting a deformation map (per vertex offsets). And the last stage refines high frequency details (wrinkes, etc) by predicting a displacement map (similar to a normal map).

I used HRN to generate approx ~10k diverse 3D face shapes. Unfortunately, that means that face expressions would be baked into the geometry, and so the transformer would be also learning those. But, in general, if the transformer learns to approximate HRN’s output well, I get a near sota solution that can be run in a single inference step (compared to HRN, 3 stages of which would take approximate 9 seconds on my RTX 4090).

Face embeddings coming into play#

To adapt Disney’s Shape Transformer architecture for 3D face reconstruction from a single photo, I made several modifications to their pipeline.

  1. Remove the encoder part Since I was working with face embeddings (which is way simpler than training training my own face encoder), I dropped the encoder entirely. Instead, the face embedding is fed directly into the decoder to condition the reconstruction process.
  2. Conditioning with Face Embeddings Inside the decoder, face embeddings are used in a couple of diffrent ways. One, they’re used for modulation between attention blocks. And also, they surve as conditioning inputs in cross attention blocks.
  3. Dynamic canonical shapes Instead of using a fixed canonical shape, I used a moprhable model trained to reconstruct face shapes from Dlib face encodings.
  4. Augmentations The more data the better, so I augmented the dataset by interpolating between pairs of samples to create new, synthetic data points. Also, random noise was added to face embeddings during training.
  5. Alongside XCiT blocks conditioned with face embeddings, I added pure XCiT blocks, to capture intra-token relationships. I saw that due to this modification the quality improvement was minimal, but the model converged significantly faster.

Conclusion#

The results appear not as impressive as those shown in the paper, but with additional training iterations, the quality should improve.

It’s important to notice one detail here: we see that face images are getting aligned, but not exactly in the expected manner. Results are aligned with each other, but they are not aligned with the canonical pose (which would mean removing the initial distortion).

One could actually achieve such result, by adding an additional supervision based on face key-points. This is actually done in the 3D case, to ensure a specific UV split.
